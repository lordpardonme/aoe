# Resume & Application Writing Standards

Standing checklist for every resume, cover note, and application email generated in this
workspace. Originated from a "10 things HR sees in a resume" social post (claimed source:
a Blinkit HR reviewer), fact-checked against real hiring/recruiting sourcing on
2026-08-19 before adoption — 8 of 10 points held up as stated, 2 were corrected. Do not
re-adopt the original unverified wording for points 2 and 10; use the corrected versions
below.

## Verification record (why each point is/isn't adopted verbatim)

| # | Original claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every bullet needs Problem → Action → Outcome | Verified | Matches documented PAR/STAR/SAR/APR frameworks (Indeed, Teal, LinkedIn, university career centers). |
| 2 | Don't 100%-match the JD, present add-ons | **Corrected** | Real 2026 sourcing: prioritize keywords that repeat/are heavily weighted in the JD first, *then* layer genuine add-ons. "Don't match" as stated risks hurting ATS parsing. |
| 3 | Quantify impact in numbers/%, not qualitative | Strongly verified | LinkedIn data: 92% of recruiters cite quantified achievements as the top shortlisting factor; ~40% more interview invites for quantified resumes. |
| 4 | Avoid generic/textbook-sounding projects | Plausible | Consistent with general specificity-over-generic advice; no dedicated study found, nothing contradicts it either. |
| 5 | "Worked with team to achieve..." needs individual ownership | Verified by extension | Same PAR/STAR sourcing explicitly wants individual action attributed, not team-generic phrasing. |
| 6 | Highlight achievements and TAT (turnaround time) | Verified, role-dependent | "Measures of time" is an explicitly valid quantification metric; most relevant for ops/delivery-heavy roles, not universal. |
| 7 | "Don't be a pick-me candidate" | Philosophy, not a documented rule | No direct source names this, but consistent with "show don't tell" advice and already matches this workspace's own AGENTS.md §6.1 voice rule. |

## The 10-point standard (corrected, in force)

1. Every bullet: Problem/Situation → Action → Outcome.
2. Prioritize JD keywords that repeat or are clearly emphasized; layer in genuine
   differentiating add-ons alongside that match, not instead of it.
3. Quantify every impact claim with a real number or percentage — never leave a claim
   purely qualitative when a number exists (see career-evidence-bank.md for the verified
   metrics on file; never invent a number that isn't grounded there).
4. No generic/copy-pasted-sounding project descriptions — specificity over template
   language.
5. Every bullet names who owned what — no "worked with team to achieve" without
   attribution.
6. Where relevant (ops/delivery/turnaround-sensitive roles), highlight TAT/time metrics
   specifically.
7. Tone shows fit through specifics, not through asking to be picked — reinforces the
   existing §6.1 email-voice rule (no "I am writing to express my keen interest").
8. Bullets only, 1-2 lines max each.
9. Clean, single visual system per resume — no template chaos.
10. Cold outreach to a recruiter = a short, personalized email, never a formal cover
    letter, and never a full resume attached unsolicited. A cover letter belongs only to a
    specific, already-open application.

## Gate procedure — mandatory before every send

This is a literal scoring gate, not a vibe check. Run it after drafting the resume, before
building the PDF:

1. Score each of the 10 points as met (1) or not-met (0) against the specific resume just
   drafted.
2. Total the score.
3. **If the total is 7 or higher**, proceed to the PDF build step as normal.
4. **If the total is below 7**, identify the specific failing points, revise the resume to
   address them, and re-score before proceeding. Do not build or send a resume scoring
   below 7/10.
5. This applies to the resume content. Apply points 7 and 10 specifically to the
   accompanying email/cover note as well.
